title: Why Private Markets
description: The structural shift that makes private market exposure one of the most important gaps in modern finance.
---

The composition of the global economy has fundamentally shifted over the last two decades. The companies defining the next era of technology, defense, finance, and consumer behavior are no longer going public when they reach scale — they are staying private, often for a decade or more.

## The structural shift

There are more large private companies today than at any point in history. They are worth more in aggregate, they employ more people, and they are generating more revenue than ever while remaining entirely outside the reach of public markets. The rise of private capital — venture, growth equity, and late-stage secondary markets — has made it possible for these companies to fund themselves without listing.

The result is a bifurcated economy: institutional investors with access to private markets can participate in this growth, while everyone else is limited to public equities that increasingly exclude the most dynamic part of the market.

## The secondary market exists — but it's fragmented

A secondary market for private company shares already exists. Platforms like Forge, Hiive, Nasdaq Private Market, and Caplight facilitate real transactions between accredited investors in pre-IPO company equity. These transactions produce real price signals — bid-ask quotes, transaction volumes, and midpoints — that reflect genuine market consensus about where these companies are headed.

The problem is that this data is fragmented across platforms, accessible only to accredited investors, and never aggregated into a continuous, comparable market signal.

## What's missing

There is no equivalent of the S&P 500 for private companies. No continuous price signal. No two-sided market where participants with different views can trade against each other. No mechanism for anyone outside the accredited investor community to express a view on private company trajectory.

Primi is built to fill that gap — not by creating synthetic exposure to private equity, but by aggregating the data that already exists and building a transparent, regulated market on top of it.
